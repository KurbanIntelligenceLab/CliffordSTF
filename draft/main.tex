\begin{abstract}
In three dimensions the geometric product of two vectors realises only the $L{=}0$ and $L{=}1$ components of the Clebsch--Gordan decomposition $\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{0}\oplus\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{2}$, leaving the five-dimensional symmetric-traceless $L{=}2$ irrep absent at the per-edge bilinear; this is the representation-theoretic reason that $\mathrm{Cl}(3,0)$ interatomic potentials predict force magnitudes accurately but force directions poorly. We close the gap with \textbf{CliffordSTF}, an architecture that runs closed-form symmetric-traceless rank-2 and rank-3 tensor tracks alongside the Clifford multivector and uses a single learned bilinear (the geometric product as an $8\times 8$ Cayley table), with no e3nn, Clebsch--Gordan tables, or Wigner-$D$ matrices. On rMD17 at a matched $10^6$-parameter budget across ten molecules, CliffordSTF raises aggregate force-cosine similarity from $0.055$ (base Clifford) to $0.551$, an order-of-magnitude relative directional gain at improved magnitude accuracy (force MAE $20.9\to17.6$\,meV/\AA{}; energy MAE $34.1\to30.4$\,meV), outperforming every CG-free or body-ordered competitor (all $\leq 0.17$ aggregate $f_\mathrm{cos}$) and narrowing the absolute gap to spherical-harmonic architectures (EquiformerV2 $0.96$ at $L{=}4$). A controlled eleven-variant ablation evidences two empirically complementary lineages, since neither the vanilla Clifford baseline nor an STF-only configuration approaches the hybrid scaffold. On catalysis, CliffordSTF attains the best in- and out-of-distribution S2EF energy MAE on OC22 in our study and the best $L\!\geq\!2$ in-distribution energy MAE and out-of-distribution EwT on OC22 IS2RE.
\end{abstract}
