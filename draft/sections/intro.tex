Machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) have emerged as practical surrogates for first-principles electronic structure calculations, enabling molecular dynamics at scales inaccessible to density functional theory \citep{unke2021machine}. A central design challenge is encoding the symmetries of physical law: energies must be invariant and forces equivariant under rotations and translations. The dominant approach constructs features from spherical harmonics composed through tensor products weighted by Clebsch--Gordan (CG) coefficients \citep{thomas2018tensor,batzner2022nequip,batatia2022mace}, but the computational cost of high-order couplings scales steeply with the maximum angular momentum order $L$, motivating both efficient implementations \citep{geiger2022e3nn,passaro2023escn} and CG-free reformulations based on irreducible Cartesian tensors \citep{simeon2023tensornet,zaverkin2024higher,wang2024n}.

Clifford (geometric) algebra offers an independent CG-free path. Prior architectures embed vectors into the eight-dimensional algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(3,0)$ and replace learned tensor products with the single closed-form geometric product, which handles scalars, vectors, bivectors, and pseudoscalars while preserving equivariance \citep{ruhe2023cgenn,ruhe2023gcan,brehmer2023geometric,brandstetter2022clifford}. A fundamental expressivity limitation of this substrate has nevertheless gone unacknowledged. The geometric product of two vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$ yields a scalar and a bivector, together realising only the $L{=}0$ and $L{=}1$ components of $\mathbf{1} \otimes \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0} \oplus \mathbf{1} \oplus \mathbf{2}$ at the per-edge bilinear used in every message-passing layer; the five-dimensional symmetric traceless $L{=}2$ component never enters the feature substrate directly. Features required for quadrupolar distributions, $d$-orbital interactions, and anisotropic stress must therefore be reconstructed indirectly through repeated products and many stacked layers, placing Clifford backbones at a systematic disadvantage against spherical-harmonic architectures on atomic systems.

Here we introduce, CliffordSTF, to close the gap by supplying the missing irreps directly. Two Cartesian primitives, a symmetric traceless rank-2 tensor and its rank-3 derivative, run alongside the Clifford multivector on independent tracks and couple back into it through bilinear contractions corresponding bijectively to specific CG channels. The architecture uses one core bilinear, the geometric product as an $8 \times 8$ Cayley table, augmented by closed-form symmetrisation and trace removal, with no spherical harmonics, Wigner-$D$ matrices, CG tables, or e3nn calls. Across five benchmarks and up to twelve baselines evaluated under a controlled protocol at matched parameter budget, adding the STF tracks to the Clifford baseline improves the rMD17 force cosine similarity by an order of magnitude ($0.055$ to $0.551$) while also reducing force and energy mean absolute errors, outperforms a fully-configured EquiformerV2 at $L{=}4$ on OC22 IS2RE, and attains the best out-of-distribution S2EF energy MAE in the study, while an eleven-variant ablation isolates the $L \geq 2$ scaffold as the dominant driver of directional accuracy with cross-track coupling providing training stability. Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Theory.} We identify the empirical cost of the $L \geq 2$ gap of the geometric product in $\mathrm{Cl}(3,0)$ networks and show that the geometric product plus closed-form symmetric traceless tensor operations span the full Clebsch--Gordan decomposition for irrep tensor products through $L{=}3$ at the per-layer bilinear, realising at the architectural level the classical Cartesian--spherical equivalence of irreducible tensors without runtime use of CG coefficient tables, spherical harmonics, or Wigner-$D$ matrices; numerical verification against Wigner $3j$-symbols confirms equivalence to machine precision.
    \item \textbf{Architecture.} We propose CliffordSTF, in which Clifford multivector channels and STF channels couple through bilinear cross-track operations corresponding bijectively to specific CG channels, with a block-diagonal linear map, equivariant gating, and a dual Clifford/Hodge force readout.
    \item \textbf{Empirics.} CliffordSTF improves on the Clifford baseline on force magnitude, energy, and directional fidelity simultaneously on rMD17, outperforms a fully-configured EquiformerV2 at $L{=}4$ on OC22 IS2RE, and attains the best out-of-distribution S2EF energy MAE in our study. An eleven-variant ablation shows that the $L \geq 2$ scaffold closes most of the directional gap; individual flag toggles affect energy-MAE stability and seed-to-seed variance more than headline cosine similarity.
\end{itemize}
